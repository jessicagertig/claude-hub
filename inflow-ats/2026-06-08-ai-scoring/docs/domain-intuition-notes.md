# Domain Intuition: How JDs Work Across Roles

## Customer-facing responsibilities use different vocabulary by tier

Entry-level roles (Host, Housekeeper) describe customer interaction as attitude and demeanor: "friendly," "positive," "patience and customer-oriented approach." Mid-level roles (Team Lead, Sales Manager) frame it as process ownership: "resolve complaints," "monitor performance," "build long-term customer relationships." Senior roles (VP Service & Operations, Director of Development) frame it as strategy: "implement best practices in client service," "build and maintain relationships with key supporters." The underlying skill is the same -- managing people's expectations and emotions -- but the language shifts from personal temperament to operational capability to strategic vision. A hospitality host and a customer support rep share more transferable skill than their JDs suggest.

## Soft skills are universal but weighted differently

Every single JD mentions communication. Leadership shows up even in non-management roles (Video Production: "works with outside crew"; Housekeeper: "report it, tell us"). Problem-solving appears in technical roles explicitly ("strong analytical and problem-solving skills") and in service roles implicitly ("deal with complaints or problems with a positive attitude"). The real differentiator is autonomy: entry-level roles emphasize following checklists and taking direction; mid-level roles emphasize self-management and independent judgment; senior roles emphasize setting direction for others. When scoring, treat autonomy-related language as a proxy for seniority fit.

## Transferable experience follows hidden bridges

Hospitality (host, housekeeper) maps directly to customer service through guest interaction, complaint handling, and multitasking under pressure. Sales experience (PharmD Strategist, Sales Manager) transfers across industries when the sales methodology is similar (B2C vs B2B, transactional vs consultative). Project management transfers to any leadership role. Nonprofit fundraising (Director of Development) explicitly says "development, sales, or a similar role" -- treating fundraising and sales as equivalent. Creative roles (Video Production, Graphic Designer) transfer on tool proficiency more than on industry. CRM experience (HubSpot, Salesforce, OpenTable) transfers across any role that manages relationships or pipelines.

## Tools are named specifically or by category depending on role criticality

When a tool IS the job, JDs name it: SolidWorks for mechanical engineering, Adobe Creative Cloud for video/design, stenographic software for CART captioning. When a tool supports the job, JDs name categories: "CRM tools like HubSpot," "customer service software and systems," "industry technology platforms." Knowing the named tool is a strong signal; knowing the category is a moderate signal; having no tool experience in a tool-critical role is a genuine gap, not just a nice-to-have miss.

## Years of experience encode seniority expectations, not hard cutoffs

Entry-level roles (Host, Housekeeper) specify zero years. Specialist roles ask 2-5 years (PharmD Strategist, CART Captioner, Mechanical Engineer). Mid-management asks 3-5 years (Team Lead, Social Media Manager). Senior/director roles ask 5-8+ years (VP Operations, Director of Development, Enterprise AE). But the real signal is scope: a candidate with 4 years managing a 20-person team is a stronger Team Lead fit than someone with 7 years as an individual contributor. Years are a proxy for depth of practiced judgment.

## Certifications gate entry; education validates domain knowledge

Hard certification gates are rare but absolute: CART Captioner requires RPR/RMR/RDR/CRC/CRR. PharmD Strategist requires a PharmD degree. FINRA licenses are "preferred" for VP Operations. Most roles say "degree preferred" or "equivalent experience." Mechanical Engineering requires a degree because the knowledge is non-negotiable. Creative and customer service roles treat portfolios and demonstrated ability as degree-equivalent. When a certification is listed as required, it is a true binary filter; when education is listed as preferred, practical experience in the domain should count at near-equal weight.
